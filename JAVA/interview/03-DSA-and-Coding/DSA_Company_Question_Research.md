# Company DSA question research: evidence boundary

## What is established

No company-specific DSA question or encounter-frequency estimate is accepted in Phase 2. The exercises in this module are original, company-neutral teaching tasks. Official hiring guides can establish coding expectations within their role scope; they do not establish that an employer asks a particular public problem.

[Phase 1 company findings](../01-Company-Research/README.md) preserve inspected guidance and gaps. A company's use of graphs, payments or machine learning in its products does not prove that its interviews emphasize a corresponding problem pattern.

## A usable evidence record

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Claim | Narrow statement: a specific public account reports a task, not “the company always asks” |
| Employer / role / level / location | Preserve known scope; use unknown instead of inventing |
| Interview date / report date | Distinguish event from publication; a recent repost can describe an old event |
| Source URL / access date | Readable original account where possible |
| Label | OFFICIAL, CANDIDATE-REPORTED, COMMUNITY-TREND, INFERRED or UNVERIFIED |
| Task description | Nonconfidential high-level summary; no leaked/private question reproduction |
| Corroboration | Independent sources, not mirrors or copies of one account |
| Limits | Sample bias, uncertain role, stale date, inaccessible original, platform methodology |

## Why frequency claims are difficult

Company tags may be based on user reports, platform history or an undisclosed collection method. They can change over time, mix levels/locations and omit the number of candidates who did not encounter the task. A list ranked “frequent” is not a calibrated probability of an interview question. Paywalls and inaccessible data are not a reason to invent rankings.

Candidate accounts are selected samples: motivated posters, remembered questions, uneven geography and successful/failed outcomes. Two matching stories may be the same copied account. Even independent repetition supports only a scoped observation unless a defined sampling method supports broader conclusions.

## Practical preparation decision

[INFERRED] Use patterns and unfamiliar variants as the core. For an upcoming interview, obtain the current role-specific guide and permitted-tool policy, then practice the supported format. If reliable public reports exist, use their high-level topics as supplementary signals and retain their uncertainty. Do not spend most preparation time trying to predict one exact question.

## Next research task

During full company dossier work, collect dated public accounts for the actual target role and region, record independent sample size and contradictions, and link them through the source index. Leave question-frequency and current-loop claims unverified if the necessary evidence remains unavailable.
