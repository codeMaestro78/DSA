# Projects in behavioral interviews

## Connect decisions and collaboration

A project can support technical and behavioral evidence. In a behavioral answer, focus on a decision, disagreement, ambiguity or learning episode; retain enough technical detail to explain why the decision mattered. A catalog of frameworks does not show your behavior.

## Followup chain

Start with a real project and ask:

1. Who needed it and what was your responsibility?
2. What was the most uncertain requirement?
3. Which alternatives did you consider?
4. Who disagreed and what concern did they raise?
5. What did you personally implement or investigate?
6. How was the result checked?
7. What did the project not prove?
8. What would you change under a different constraint?

These are original [INFERRED] practice prompts, not company-attributed questions.

## Evidence types

A repository shows implementation. Tests show behavior within their cases and environment. A local load test describes one workload. Production monitoring can describe an observed deployment and period. A screenshot shows a rendered state. None automatically establishes the others. Use accurate verbs: implemented, inspected, tested, measured, deployed or observed.

## Explain a team result

Say “I implemented the retrieval filters; another teammate owned ingestion; we evaluated [actual sample].” Do not say “I built the whole system” when responsibilities were shared. If you used an AI coding assistant, explain what it helped with and how you verified the result rather than claiming unexamined output as expertise.

## Practice

Choose one resume bullet and build a claim → artifact/measurement → limitation table. Prepare a two-minute answer centered on the decision behind that bullet. Then let a reviewer challenge the strongest claim.

<details><summary>Review criteria</summary>

A reviewer can distinguish your contribution, team work, actual evidence and hypothetical improvement. You can explain the chosen technology through a constraint and name a credible rejected alternative.

</details>

## Checklist

- [ ] Project context and contribution are precise.
- [ ] Evidence supports the resume wording.
- [ ] Followups do not require invented results.
