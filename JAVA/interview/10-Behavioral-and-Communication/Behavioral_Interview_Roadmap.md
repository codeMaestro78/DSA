# Behavioral preparation roadmap

## Goal

Build a small set of truthful episodes you can explain under followup. This is original [INFERRED] preparation guidance. It is not a list of questions guaranteed at any company.

## Four-session progression

| Session | Work | Output and readiness check |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Inventory real episodes from work, study, open source or volunteering | Six one-line episodes, each labeled with actual context and your role |
| 2 | Develop STAR plus reflection | One paragraph of actions, one observed result and one lesson per episode |
| 3 | Test with followups | A reviewer can distinguish your decisions, alternatives and team contributions |
| 4 | Tailor and rehearse | Two-minute responses answering the specific prompt without changing facts |

Suggested themes: ownership, disagreement, failure, ambiguity, prioritization and user impact. A single episode may support several themes, but select the facts relevant to the question. Do not force a success story to answer “Tell me about a failure” while hiding the consequence.

## Evidence ledger

For each episode record date/context, task, your action, collaborators, result evidence, what you did not measure, confidentiality limits and later behavior change. Use qualitative evidence when quantitative measurement does not exist. “The reviewer accepted the fix after these tests” is supportable if true; “improved performance by 80%” requires an actual comparable measurement.

## Preparation sequence

Read [STAR](STAR_Method.md), then [ownership](Ownership.md), [conflict](Conflict_Resolution.md), [failure](Failure_and_Learning.md) and [the question bank](Behavioral_Question_Bank.md). Use [project deep dives](Project_Deep_Dive.md) when a behavioral story needs technical followup.

## Practice

Ask a reviewer to interrupt with “Why?”, “What did you do?” and “What would you change?” A response that only works without interruption is over-rehearsed. Practice pauses and corrections rather than memorizing every sentence.

<details><summary>Readiness criteria</summary>

You answer the question directly, preserve factual consistency, name a personal decision and support the outcome. You can discuss a mistake and a reasonable alternative without blaming others or pretending certainty. A lack of paid experience is handled by accurately identifying the context of the example.

</details>

## Checklist

- [ ] Six true episodes, not six fabricated ideal answers.
- [ ] Evidence and limits recorded.
- [ ] Followups practiced and one lesson applied later.
