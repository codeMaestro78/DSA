# Resume claim verification

## Treat each bullet as a claim

For each statement, identify what you did, its scope, the evidence and the strongest likely followup. Prefer precise contributions over broad skill labels. This is [INFERRED] preparation guidance; it does not prescribe one employer's resume format.

| Claim type | Evidence to prepare | Boundary to state |
|---|---|---|
| Built an API | Routes/contracts, validation, tests and your implementation role | Local prototype versus deployed service |
| Improved performance | Before/after workload, measurement method and variability | Cache state, hardware and scope of measurement |
| Built RAG | Ingestion/retrieval flow, permissions and evaluated answers | Demonstration versus validated quality on a defined dataset |
| Led a team | Decisions, coordination and teammate responsibilities | Leadership activity versus formal management authority |
| Used ML | Data split, baseline, metric and experiment record | Training a model versus invoking an API |

## Quantification

A percentage needs a denominator and comparable conditions. A response-time reduction measured on ten local requests should not become a company-wide production impact claim. When no reliable measurement exists, describe the function delivered or the verification performed rather than inventing a number.

## Explain unfamiliar technology honestly

If a bullet names Redis, be ready to explain why it was used, what data it held, whether it was authoritative, and how failures or expiry affected behavior. If the usage was limited, say so. A technology's presence in a dependency file is weaker evidence than implementation and operational understanding.

## Practice

Read each bullet and ask “How do you know?” Then ask “What part was yours?” Revise ambiguous wording before rehearsing a polished answer. Remove details you cannot share; describe the general constraint instead of revealing private data.

<details><summary>Readiness check</summary>

Every strong claim has an artifact or observation you can explain, and its limits are clear. You can answer one layer deeper than the bullet without switching to generic product documentation.

</details>

## Checklist

- [ ] Role, scale and environment are accurate.
- [ ] Metrics are traceable and comparable.
- [ ] Team and personal contributions are separate.
- [ ] Confidential details are excluded.
