# Conflict and technical disagreement

## Describe the disputed decision

A useful conflict story is about different goals, constraints or beliefs and how you worked toward a decision. Avoid diagnosing a colleague's personality. Say what each option optimized and what evidence was available.

## A practical reasoning sequence

[INFERRED] Clarify the shared objective, restate the other person's concern fairly, identify the decision owner, compare alternatives under the same constraints, gather a small useful piece of evidence, make the decision and follow through. Escalation can be appropriate when authority, safety or commitments exceed your scope; explain why and whom you involved.

## Adaptable technical scenario

A teammate proposes an asynchronous job for a slow request; you propose optimizing the query first. A strong discussion asks whether the user needs an immediate result, whether a slow query is measured, what retries would mean and what complexity a worker introduces. An experiment could compare query plans and latency on a representative local fixture. That experiment informs the technical decision; it does not automatically establish production behavior.

This is a hypothetical exercise. If using a real episode, replace it with the actual alternatives and evidence rather than claiming this scenario happened to you.

## Answer followups honestly

“What changed your mind?” requires a specific observation or argument. “What if you still disagreed?” requires a description of the decision process and how you acted afterward. Constructive support does not mean concealing a serious unresolved risk. Describe how you documented and communicated that risk.

## Common mistakes

Declaring the other person incompetent, skipping their legitimate concern, overstating your authority, claiming to have “convinced everyone” without evidence, or ending before implementation and outcome. A disagreement you lost can still demonstrate good judgment and collaboration.

## Practice

Use one real disagreement. State the alternative you favored and then make the strongest fair case for the other option.

<details><summary>Readiness check</summary>

A reviewer understands the shared goal, the tension, your action and the result. Your account would remain recognizable if the colleague heard it. The lesson changes a future behavior, such as agreeing on success criteria before debating technologies.

</details>

## Checklist

- [ ] Disputed decision and shared goal clear.
- [ ] Other perspective represented fairly.
- [ ] Evidence, decision authority and follow-through explained.
