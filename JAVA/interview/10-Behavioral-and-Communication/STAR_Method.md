# STAR and reflection

## The structure

[OFFICIAL] Microsoft recommends STAR(R): situation, task, action, result and reflection. [Interview tips](https://careers.microsoft.com/v2/global/en/hiring-tips/interview-tips). The allocations and examples below are [INFERRED] writing aids, not employer scoring rules.

| Part | Question it answers | Keep it concrete |
|---|---|---|
| Situation | What was happening? | One event and relevant constraint |
| Task | What responsibility was yours? | Scope, goal and your decision authority |
| Action | What did you do and why? | Alternatives, personal steps, coordination and checks |
| Result | What happened? | Observed outcome, including shortfalls |
| Reflection | What did you learn or change? | A specific later behavior, not a slogan |

For a two-minute rehearsal, spend most of the time on action and reasoning. A long company history leaves no time to explain your decision. This is flexible: a complex context may need an extra sentence.

## Adaptable answer scaffold

This is a form to fill with real facts, not a personal experience to claim:

“In [real context], [specific problem] affected [user/workflow]. I was responsible for [scope]. I considered [A] and [B], and chose [choice] because [constraint]. I personally [action] and coordinated [other action] with [person/team]. We checked [evidence]. The outcome was [actual result], with [limit]. I later changed [specific practice].”

## Improve a weak answer

Weak: “The project was difficult, but I worked hard and we succeeded.” It omits the task, decision and evidence. Improve it by identifying one decision under one constraint, then describing how you checked its effect. Do not improve it by inventing numbers.

## Questions and followups

What if the result was poor? What if the action was shared?

<details><summary>Expected reasoning</summary>

A poor result can be a valid failure story if you explain the consequences, your responsibility and correction. For shared work, name your contribution and credit the team accurately. STAR structures evidence; it does not require taking sole credit or manufacturing success.

</details>

## Checklist

- [ ] Each part answers a different question.
- [ ] Actions contain reasons, not only activity.
- [ ] Result and reflection are factual and specific.
