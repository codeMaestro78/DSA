# Ownership

## Ownership as observable work

This chapter uses an [INFERRED] practice definition: ownership means taking appropriate responsibility for moving a problem toward a verified outcome while coordinating with the people who have authority and expertise. It does not require doing everything alone or making unauthorized changes.

## Explain the complete chain

Need noticed → scope clarified → action chosen → stakeholders informed → result checked → remaining work handed off. A story ending at “I submitted the patch” leaves acceptance, rollout and effect unknown. If you were responsible only for the patch, say so and explain what the actual owner verified next.

## A useful early-career example structure

“In [real project], I noticed [specific problem]. I confirmed it using [evidence]. Although my assignment was [scope], I raised [issue] and agreed [bounded next step]. I implemented [personal contribution], requested [review] and checked [result]. [Other person/team] owned [remaining responsibility].”

This is an adaptable form, not an invented episode. A small example with clear evidence is stronger preparation than a grand claim that cannot survive followup.

## Tradeoffs to discuss

How did you prioritize this issue against existing work? What was reversible? Who needed to approve an action? How did you know the problem was resolved? When did you ask for help? Ownership includes recognizing a limit before a risky change, not only persistence afterward.

## Common weak answers

“I worked the hardest” provides activity but little decision evidence. “I fixed another team's service without telling them” may hide an authority and coordination problem. “We delivered everything” obscures your role. Correct these by naming a decision, agreed scope and checked outcome.

## Practice

Choose a real instance where you followed up after your first action. Explain why the initial action was insufficient and what closed the loop.

<details><summary>Review criteria</summary>

The reviewer can identify what you owned, what others owned, the evidence for completion and any remaining limit. Taking credit for the entire result when your role was narrower does not meet the criterion.

</details>

## Checklist

- [ ] Scope and authority stated.
- [ ] Action connected to a user or system need.
- [ ] Verification and handoff described.
