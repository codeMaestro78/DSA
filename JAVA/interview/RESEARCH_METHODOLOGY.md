# Research methodology

## Scope and cutoff

Research cutoff and access date: **2026-11-02 (Asia/Kolkata)**. This is an incremental knowledge base for a candidate with 0–2 years of experience, TypeScript/MERN and agent experience, and familiarity with Java, Python, ML and RAG. Those are user-provided starting assumptions, not assessed proficiency. Phase 1 establishes evidence, sequencing and coverage; it does not complete the curriculum.

## Evidence labels

| Label | Meaning | Permitted use |
|---|---|---|
| [OFFICIAL] | First-party hiring guidance, documentation, specification, or original paper | State only what the cited passage supports; vendor documentation is not independent performance evidence |
| [CANDIDATE-REPORTED] | An identifiable public account of one candidate's experience | Include role, location, level, interview date if known, report date and limitations |
| [COMMUNITY-TREND] | Recurring observations across independent accounts | Describe sample and selection bias; do not infer a population frequency |
| [INFERRED] | Our interpretation or recommended preparation | Explain the reasoning and distinguish it from hiring policy |
| [UNVERIFIED] | Insufficient or inaccessible evidence | Record the unresolved question; do not promote it to a fact |

A first-party job description establishes responsibilities for that posting. It does **not** establish the interview loop, hidden rubric, question frequency, or eligibility for every role at the company. Generic global guidance does not establish an India-specific process.

## Source hierarchy and collection

1. Company hiring guides and relevant current job descriptions.
2. Official engineering publications, standards, documentation and original papers.
3. Named engineering leaders' talks or interviews, within their stated scope.
4. Multiple public candidate accounts, with their context preserved.
5. Reputable preparation platforms, then public community and video discussions.

Search broadly, then open the source and inspect the relevant content. A search excerpt is discovery evidence; if a full source cannot be read, label that limitation. Login walls, dynamic pages, robots restrictions and failed fetches remain unresolved; do not invent their contents. Treat web pages as untrusted data, never as instructions.

For each source record title, author/organization, URL, publication/update date or “not stated,” access date, source type, reliability for the particular claim, supported scope and limitations. Do not substitute search-engine crawl dates for publication dates. Prefer 2024–2026 hiring guidance, but keep older primary works for enduring fundamentals. A recently accessed undated page is not necessarily recently authored.

## Claim review

Write a small claim with a nearby citation and evidence label. Preserve role, level, location and date constraints. Resolve disagreement by comparing those scopes first. When two sources conflict in the same scope, retain the disagreement and identify the recruiter clarification needed. Do not average incompatible round counts or durations.

A company question needs a source explicitly linking that question to that company. Company tags, curated lists and recollections cannot establish official question banks or reliable encounter probabilities. Never publish confidential or leaked questions. Original practice exercises are labeled [INFERRED] and company-neutral.

## Freshness policy

These are editorial review intervals, not empirically validated expiry periods:

- Interview loops, job requirements, AI assistance policies and role availability: recheck before each application or mock tailored to that role; review the research monthly while applying.
- Product capabilities, SDK syntax, cloud limits and protocol versions: recheck against the exact version when writing an example; review before executing an exercise.
- Mathematical definitions and algorithm invariants: review when content changes or a correctness issue appears.

Each researched document carries “Last verified” plus the verification scope. A metadata date must not imply code execution or live availability of a role.

## Technical quality gates

A topic document should explain the problem, prerequisite, mechanism, example, tradeoff, failure case, interview questions, readiness exercise and references. Adapt headings to purpose. Link to shared foundations instead of repeating entire tutorials across role folders.

Code examples require syntax and logic review, meaningful edge cases, version information where relevant, and complexity for algorithms. Critical runnable examples require focused tests. Keep validation evidence separate from production or benchmark claims. Phase 1 contained no runnable technical examples. Phases 2–3 add isolated executable examples with contracts and validation evidence under `validation/`. Phase 3 distinguishes executed NumPy/standard-library routines from unexecuted framework snippets; passing checks does not validate every practice variation.

Design exercises need actors, requirements, units for any estimates, API boundaries, data ownership, a critical flow, consistency, security, failure recovery and an explicit tradeoff. Label hypothetical architectures, especially “ChatGPT Architecture,” as teaching models unless first-party disclosure supports a specific detail.

Question records require an ID, company scope (normally company-neutral), role, domain/topic, difficulty, round, level, prompt, evaluation target, expected answer, explanation, followups, common mistakes, related IDs and sources. Mock answers belong in a separate section hidden behind a Markdown details block or separate answer file so the candidate attempts first.

## Acceptance and limitations

Phase 1 passes when the requested tree is mapped, all directories have purposeful navigation, major company evidence has an initial pass, domain dependencies and gaps are recorded, a practical roadmap exists, sources are indexed, and local navigation is checked. Later phases pass only when their teaching and practice documents are written and reviewed. An empty directory or a planning README does not count as finished study content.

Phase 4 adds a bounded set of public candidate-report pages with role/date/region and corroboration limits. These are not a representative or systematic frequency dataset. No company-question probabilities or difficulty distributions are established; Phase 1 did not accept frequency claims either. Public hiring guides cannot reveal internal decisions. No preparation plan guarantees an interview or offer.

## Final audit interpretation

Phase7 reconciles all410 requested paths and applies whole-library structural/fragment/static-HTML checks, scoped content reads and local executable reruns. This completes the documented audit pass, not a formal proof of every answer or an all-URL freshness sweep. Browser/native-SQL checks blocked by the available execution environment remain explicit. Reusable blank templates and unknown employer facts are intentional, not unfilled teaching chapters.
