# Big tech: Phase 1 evidence findings

Research cutoff and last verification: **2026-11-02**. This is an initial evidence review, not a complete company dossier. Scope is public hiring guidance; no private question banks or alleged leaked questions are used. Company guidance does not establish an identical process across locations, teams, or levels. Links below were read unless an access limitation is explicitly recorded.

## Google

- [OFFICIAL] Google's virtual-interview guide says the recruiting coordinator sends the interview schedule and Google Meet link. It recommends testing equipment and explains that the tools used depend on the interview. [G-BT-01](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/general_virtual_interviews_candidate_resource.pdf)
- [OFFICIAL] The same guide prohibits using AI to answer interview questions and requires candidates to submit their own work. Confirm the latest instructions for the particular invitation. [G-BT-01](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/general_virtual_interviews_candidate_resource.pdf)
- [OFFICIAL] Google's technical virtual-interview guide describes Google Drawings for **some design questions**, with guidance on communicating diagrams. This does not establish that every early-career SWE applicant receives a design round. [G-BT-02](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/technical_virtual_interviews_candidate_resource.pdf)
- [INFERRED] Preparation: rehearse spoken coding explanations and a small architecture diagram over video; practice without AI assistance. Ask the recruiter which coding tool, language, and design format apply.
- Gap: the main hiring-process page returned navigation but no usable process body in this research tool. Round counts, duration, hiring-committee rules, team matching, India-specific differences, and early-career design requirements remain unverified here. Do not substitute an old mirrored guide for current proof.

## Amazon

- [OFFICIAL] Amazon's software-development guidance describes an SDE online assessment including a coding test; it also says most technical interviews involve coding and system-design whiteboarding. It explicitly recommends consulting the recruiter about the applicable subjects. [G-BT-03](https://www.amazon.jobs/content/en/how-we-hire/interview-prep/software-development-topics)
- [OFFICIAL] Its preparation topics include algorithms, data structures, object-oriented design, databases, distributed computing, operating systems, internet topics, and general ML/AI. The emphasis is applying fundamentals, with practice outside an IDE. [G-BT-03](https://www.amazon.jobs/content/en/how-we-hire/interview-prep/software-development-topics)
- [OFFICIAL] The broader hiring page states that process differs by role and provides separate resources for SDE II, SDE III, applied scientist, and research scientist candidates. A senior guide must not be silently applied to a graduate applicant. [G-BT-04](https://www.amazon.jobs/content/en/how-we-hire)
- [INFERRED] Preparation: allocate daily time to DSA plus testing, and prepare truthful project stories explaining customer impact. Use the exact role's behavioral guidance before mapping stories to Leadership Principles.
- Recruiter questions: Is this university or experienced hiring? Which assessment applies? What design depth and behavioral format should I expect? Does the process differ for this India requisition?
- Gap: no exact round count, assessment duration, pass threshold, or company-specific DSA question is established in this file.

## Microsoft

- [OFFICIAL] Technical interview guidance covers problem solving, design, coding, and testing. It asks candidates to clarify ambiguity and plan before implementation; code should be executable rather than pseudocode, using a language the candidate knows well. [G-BT-05](https://careers.microsoft.com/v2/global/en/hiring-tips/technical-interviewing)
- [OFFICIAL] Testing guidance includes boundaries, errors, security implications, and stress cases. Its role-dependent preparation list includes algorithms/data structures, distributed systems, AI/ML, data science, and networking; it is representative rather than exhaustive. [G-BT-05](https://careers.microsoft.com/v2/global/en/hiring-tips/technical-interviewing)
- [INFERRED] Preparation: practice a complete cycle of clarifying, implementing, running tests, and explaining complexity. For AI roles, add an example of evaluating or optimizing a model pipeline rather than memorizing model names.
- Recruiter questions: Which skills are evaluated for this requisition? Can I run code? Which platform is used? Is there a dedicated design, ML, or data-science discussion?
- Gap: exact early-career/India round composition and team-specific ML exercises are not established by the general technical page.

## NVIDIA

- [OFFICIAL] Hiring guidance describes meetings with the hiring manager, team members, and other groups; technical applicants may receive a coding exercise, usually through HackerRank. It says unapproved outside tools, including ChatGPT, disqualify candidates. [G-BT-06](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/about-nvidia/careers/how-we-hire/)
- [OFFICIAL] The page emphasizes role-dependent interviews and says teams can use different processes. It describes typical individual interview sessions as 30–60 minutes, not a universal end-to-end loop length. [G-BT-06](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/about-nvidia/careers/how-we-hire/)
- Accuracy concern: the page's main section requires an onsite interview before an offer, while its FAQ allows virtual or in-person stages and says interns typically have phone interviews only. Ask the recruiter which onsite policy and exceptions apply; do not erase this apparent scope difference.
- [INFERRED] Preparation: choose a target specialty first. A CUDA or systems role needs different domain preparation from an AI application role. Rehearse the algorithms and systems concepts relevant to the actual job description.
- Recruiter questions: What technical specialty is assessed? Which languages and tools are approved? Is an onsite stage required for this employment type and location?
- Gap: no universal CUDA requirement, coding difficulty distribution, or question list is established.

## Databricks

- [OFFICIAL] The April 2025 engineering preparation PDF distinguishes coding, algorithms, systems programming, architecture, domain deep dive, and cross-functional interviews for back-end engineering. Coding guidance emphasizes functional code, organization, tests, edge cases, and complexity; systems programming covers concurrency and I/O. [G-BT-07](https://www.databricks.com/sites/default/files/2025-04/engineering-careers-site-interview-prep-april-2025-002.pdf)
- [OFFICIAL] The PDF gives a back-end process example including a recruiter screen, technical screen, panel, internal reviews, references, and offer. It should be treated as a dated role-specific guide, not a guaranteed current route for every team. [G-BT-07](https://www.databricks.com/sites/default/files/2025-04/engineering-careers-site-interview-prep-april-2025-002.pdf)
- [OFFICIAL] The early-career page separately advises SWE candidates to review data structures and algorithms, with code quality/cleanliness evaluated. It explicitly says expectations vary by role. [G-BT-08](https://www.databricks.com/company/careers/university-recruiting)
- [INFERRED] Preparation: prioritize DSA and code quality for early career; add deeper concurrency, storage, and architecture according to the recruiter-confirmed track. Explain a real project with enough implementation detail for a domain discussion.
- Recruiter questions: Does the 2025 guide apply to this early-career position? Which panel subjects are included? Is systems programming part of the loop? Which language and coding environment should I prepare?
- Gap: exact current early-career panel size, India differences, and database-specialist requirements need requisition-specific confirmation.

## Stripe

- [OFFICIAL] The careers FAQ says most processes include a recruiter conversation, a technical or skills-based assessment, and team interviews. It states that timing varies by role, level, and location. [G-BT-09](https://stripe.com/careers)
- [OFFICIAL] Stripe's engineering-organization guide describes a historical Bug Squash exercise using a real open-source bug and describes standardized rubrics and candidate preparation guides. This is evidence of the published practice, not proof that every current Stripe loop contains that exercise. [G-BT-10](https://stripe.com/guides/atlas/scaling-eng)
- [INFERRED] Preparation: combine coding fundamentals with reading unfamiliar code, writing regression tests, debugging, and explaining API/error-handling decisions. Ask for the current engineering candidate resource before assuming named rounds.
- Recruiter questions: Which assessment applies? Is there a debugging or integration exercise? Can I use my own environment? What documentation or AI tools are permitted?
- Gap: no present-day universal round list or named company question is established; an undated organizational essay alone cannot establish current tool policy.

## Cross-company implications

These are preparation decisions, **[INFERRED]**, not employer policies:

1. Build coding correctness, testing, communication, and complexity reasoning before optimizing for company tags.
2. Maintain separate records for SWE, AI/ML, research, and infrastructure requisitions. A company's technical documentation is not evidence of its interview questions.
3. Confirm tool rules independently for every interview. Published policies and interview environments differ.
4. For 0–2 years of experience, use the early-career guide when available. Do not inflate the curriculum based solely on a senior back-end panel.
