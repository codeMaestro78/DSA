# Interview lifecycle

## What the stages mean

An interview process gathers evidence about a particular role. A stage can evaluate several skills, and a skill can be assessed in several stages. “Technical screen” does not uniquely mean DSA, and “onsite” can describe a block of interviews rather than its physical location. Use the invitation's actual wording.

[OFFICIAL] Microsoft says next steps vary with the role and directs candidates to recruiters for format and timeline. [Interview tips](https://careers.microsoft.com/v2/global/en/hiring-tips/interview-tips). The sequence below is an [INFERRED] planning model, not Microsoft's or every employer's fixed process.

| Possible stage | Evidence it can gather | What to prepare or confirm |
|---|---|---|
| Application review | Relevance of experience and eligibility | Truthful resume, exact requisition, location and level |
| Recruiter discussion | Motivation, logistics, scope and alignment | Concise introduction, availability, questions about format |
| Assessment/screen | Coding, domain or practical skills | Permitted language/tools, task scope, environment |
| Interview block | Multiple observations of technical and collaborative work | Role-specific practice, project evidence and followups |
| Review/decision | Comparison of evidence against role needs | Timeline and next steps; internal rules may be unpublished |
| Offer discussion | Terms and mutual expectations | Written role/location/start details and questions |

Stages may be omitted, repeated or combined. Silence alone does not establish failure, a waitlist or a hiring freeze. Ask for the expected timeline and communicate real competing deadlines accurately.

## Application record

Create one private record per requisition: company/title/location, posting URL, requirements, recruiter-confirmed stages, source/date, language/editor, AI/documentation policy, accommodations, deadline and next action. Do not publish private recruiter correspondence in this shared knowledge base.

## Worked preparation decision

A posting emphasizes backend APIs. The invitation says “live coding and project discussion” but says nothing about distributed design. Prioritize coding fluency and a defensible project walkthrough; ask whether API/design questions are included. Do not replace that evidence with a social-media claim about a different level's loop.

## Practice questions

1. What would you ask if “technical assessment” is the only description?
2. Can a public hiring guide determine your exact round count?

<details><summary>Expected reasoning</summary>

Ask about skills, format, task duration, language, code execution, allowed resources and accessibility. A public guide is an initial scope reference; the exact requisition can differ. Preserve uncertainty rather than invent stages.

</details>

## Checklist

- [ ] I know the exact role and stage, or have listed the unresolved questions.
- [ ] My calendar, time zone, platform and resource permissions are confirmed.
- [ ] I have role-relevant examples and evidence, not an assumed universal loop.
