# Hiring committees and decision reviews

## What is established

[OFFICIAL] Flipkart's public hiring guide describes feedback from interviewers being reviewed by a hiring committee with interviewers and recruiters. This is evidence about that published process; it does not establish a universal committee model. [How We Hire](https://www.flipkartcareers.com/howwehire).

Other companies may use a hiring manager, committee, panel review or another mechanism. Internal thresholds, vetoes, weighting and calibration rules are often not public. Do not claim that a particular interviewer “has final veto” without scoped evidence.

## A useful candidate mental model

[INFERRED] Treat each interview as an opportunity to provide clear evidence relevant to the role. A reviewer who did not watch your work may need to understand what you solved, what help you required, why your choices were sound and what remained incomplete. This is a preparation model, not a statement about any employer's scorecard.

Make conclusions observable: state the contract, justify the algorithm or design, test it and summarize the result. When something remains wrong, describe the defect precisely. Do not try to manipulate an imagined score rather than solve the problem collaboratively.

## Decisions cannot be reverse-engineered from silence

A delayed reply can have many causes. An unusually friendly interview does not prove an offer; a difficult followup does not prove rejection. Do not infer an employer's decision from conversation tone or crowd-sourced timelines.

Ask the recruiter for the next milestone and when a followup is appropriate. Communicate a real deadline if one exists. Afterward, record what you can improve while separating observed events from guesses about private deliberations.

| Unsupported inference to avoid | “The interviewer must have scored me below the cutoff.” |

## Practice question

Can the self-assessment rubric here predict a hiring committee's decision?

<details><summary>Expected answer</summary>

No. It organizes practice evidence and identifies weaknesses. Company needs, actual rubrics, level calibration and other evidence are outside its scope. Use it to improve observable performance, not to estimate offer odds.

</details>
